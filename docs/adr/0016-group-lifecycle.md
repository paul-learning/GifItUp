# ADR-0016: Group lifecycle (staged operations, member validation, sync rules)

- **Status:** accepted (the crate part lands with #22 PR A; bindings, snapshot v3 and the sync engine are the following #22 PRs)
- **Date:** 2026-10-26
- **Issue/PR:** #22. Round-0 plan review (SEC-1 to SEC-12). Owner decisions in chat, 2026-10-26 (question dialog): "Inside the encrypted snapshot (Recommended)"; "Read-only, then rejoin (Recommended)"; "Detect only (Recommended)"; and on members: "to me group membership (alice and bob are in a group and friends, bob adds charlie to the group, who is not alice friend yet... the group may communicate, but charlie wont show in alices contacts until she decides to add charlie to contacts), are 2 separate things".

## Context
ADR-0002 fixes MLS with the server as an untrusted Delivery Service, an envelope with device ids next to every Commit, member validation, and "mismatch means compromised, recovery is a new group". ADR-0015 fixes what the server does. Nothing yet says how a client applies commits and welcomes safely, what it checks beyond the envelope, or how sync state and freshness are kept. OpenMLS has facts that shape the answer (0.9.1, read from its source): processing a message consumes the sender's ratchet generation and stores the change at once, so the same bytes cannot be processed twice; parsing a Welcome deletes the KeyPackage it was made for; a commit with an UpdatePath replaces the committer's own leaf, and RFC 9420 leaves identity continuity to the application; a commit may carry ReInit, GroupContextExtensions, PSK and other proposals by value.

## Decision

### Staged operations (in `crypto/`, the only place MLS logic lives)
- **Our own commits are prepared, not applied.** `prepare_add / prepare_remove / prepare_update` create the commit (and the welcome) and hold it pending inside OpenMLS. The caller posts it with the ADR-0015 envelope. `merge_pending` applies it after the Delivery Service accepted it; `clear_pending` wipes it after a rejection (the storage is back to its exact earlier key set, tested). One pending commit per group, and while it is pending **no incoming commit is staged** for that group (`PendingOperation`): merging a competing commit would leave our pending commit and its path secrets behind. Incoming *application* messages of the epoch are still read (the content type is in the clear, RFC 9420 section 6.3): a committer must read what was posted before its commit, or merging the commit would make those messages undecryptable for it (tested). The engine resolves the pending commit first and skips its own echoes by device id. A pending commit **is** exported: a lost answer to an accepted commit would otherwise leave a group that can never process its own commit. On restart the engine resolves it against the server's log (merge only if the log holds our `clientMsgId` and the matching hash, else clear).
- **Incoming commits are staged and described, never applied.** `stage_incoming` returns a typed result: an application message with its **MLS-authenticated sender** (leaf username and signature key; the server's envelope is never used for "who sent this") and its message reference, or a commit as a token plus sender, added leaves, removed leaves, the epoch it applies to and whether it removes us. Only `merge_staged(token)` changes the group; `discard_staged(token)` drops it. Staging is destructive (the ratchet generation is gone), so a staged item is held **in the crate under a token** and never re-processed; at most one staged commit per group.
- **Welcomes are inspected once and joined with the token.** `inspect_welcome` parses in memory and returns the group id, the sender and every member; `join_welcome(token)` or `discard_welcome(token)` follows. The MLS group id must equal the Delivery Service group the welcome was fetched for, a group of that id must not exist locally (a welcome can never overwrite state), and the group id is 16 to 64 bytes.
- **`export_state` refuses (`PendingOperation`) while anything is staged.** A snapshot taken between staging and the decision would hold the consumed secret and no commit, so the group could never recover. Nothing is dropped silently. The engine therefore does every network and contact-book lookup **before** it stages, and after staging the decision is local and synchronous: stage, decide, merge or discard.
- **Message reference** (ADR-0006 B9): `SHA-256("GifItUp v1 message ref" || MLSMessage bytes)`, returned by `encrypt` and by the staged application result, hashed after the message parsed and processed. A regression test pins that a parsed message serialises back to the same bytes.

### What the crate refuses (no contact book needed)
- A commit may contain only **Add and Remove** proposals (by value) and its path. ReInit, GroupContextExtensions, PSK, ExternalInit, external senders and standalone proposals are `Refused` and nothing is applied. Our groups are PURE_CIPHERTEXT with external commits off.
- **Identity continuity:** no leaf's credential username or signature key may change, the committer's own new path leaf included (a self-update keeps name and key; v1 has no key rotation). A swapped name or a swapped key is `Refused`.
- Limits (ADR-0015): at most 50 leaves counting ghosts, at most 10 devices added plus removed per commit, commit and welcome at most 16 KiB, inputs checked before parsing, our own KeyPackage validation repeated at `prepare_add` (suite, lifetime, not last-resort, Basic credential with a valid username; never trusting an earlier inspection). A signature key already in the group cannot be added again.
- **Lifetime of received Adds (SEC-19):** a received Add is checked against the KeyPackage lifetime range at processing time, like our own. A member who is offline past that range, or has a skewed clock, refuses a commit the server accepted and freezes the group for itself (fail closed, recoverable by rejoin). A hostile adder could aim at that edge; we accept it, because a lenient check would let an expired KeyPackage into the group.
- **Lifetime:** the lifetime check applies to the KeyPackages we add and our own, not to existing members' leaves. A leaf that never committed keeps its KeyPackage lifetime (84 days) and would otherwise make an old group refuse every new member; the welcome is built with OpenMLS's lifetime validation skipped for the existing tree (tested with a leaf that expired).
- Staging tokens restart at 1 after a restore, and staged items are in memory only (export is refused while any exist), so a token never outlives its client; a staged welcome costs a consumed KeyPackage, which bounds how many a hostile adder can create. Closed error codes added: `PendingOperation`, `WrongEpoch`, `Replay`, `Refused`, `LimitExceeded`, `UnknownToken`. A replay is dropped silently by the engine only for a message it already knows (seq at or below the cursor, a known message reference, its own send); anything else counts as a failure.

### Members and contacts are separate (owner decision)
_What is enforced where:_ the crate (this PR) enforces identity continuity, the proposal allow-list, the limits and unique signature keys. The contact-pin check, the group-scoped first use and the envelope comparison below are **engine rules, planned in #22 PR C**; the crate does not enforce them (it accepts an Add whose username already has another leaf, a second device). A stranger's username is self-asserted by whoever added them: the UI says "added by …", never "verified".
- **Group membership and the contact book are different things.** A member added by someone I trust who is not in my contact book is a normal member of the group. They are shown as "not in your friends" (with who added them and the same emoji fingerprint available) and they **never enter my contact book on their own**; the Friends screen stays what the user decided. This amends ADR-0002's member-validation sentence ("or the contact stays blocked until the user decides") for introduced members.
- If the username **is** one of my contacts, the leaf's signature key must equal my pin; otherwise the commit is **held** (not merged) and the contact is blocked per ADR-0014. For a username that is not a contact, the first signature key seen for that name in the group is remembered for the group (group-scoped first use, as in plan v2), and a later change of a leaf's key or name is refused by identity continuity. The crate does not refuse an Add whose username already has another leaf (a second device of the same account, ADR-0015 allows several): the engine does not guess whether that key belongs to the person, and needs no server lookup for it. It holds the leaf or shows it as "new, unverified device" until the user decides (contacts: the pin, ADR-0014). A welcome that forks a new member into a group is covered by the same roster comparison (PR C); the crate only guarantees that keys are never repeated and that names and keys of existing leaves never change. **Acceptance criterion for PR C (SEC-18):** for a stranger there is no device-to-key lookup, so a second leaf with a known name and another key must not be shown as that person's verified device: the engine holds it or shows it as "new, unverified device", and a test pins this; the threat model gets the row when PR C lands. The trust is "added by a group member", which is weaker than a friend I compared emojis with, and the UI says so.
- The envelope is advisory (ADR-0015), so the client's envelope check is count- and key-based and needs no device-to-key lookup for strangers: the numbers of added and removed leaves equal the envelope's lists, removed leaves belong to the group's roster (a ghost may be removed), and added leaves follow the rules above. The warning on a mismatch names nobody ("this group's membership data does not match"): a hostile server can forge a mismatch to frame a member (changes ADR-0002's wording).

### Receive side as built (#22 PR C1, `shared/.../domain/groups`)
- **One snapshot per step.** After every applied message the engine sets the app state (cursors, statuses, failure counts, quarantine, unverified keys; strict binary codec, `SyncStateCodec`), exports the snapshot and saves it in one piece, then acknowledges once per page. A crash between storing and acknowledging fetches the page again and skips what the cursor already covers; a failed store halts with `StorageFailed` (drop the client, restore, fetch again). The message sink must be durable when `deliver` returns and tolerate a repeat.
- **Strictly consecutive.** A message is applied only at `cursor + 1`; a page that starts later than that, a jump inside a page, or a stale epoch is a gap and freezes the group. The sender label of a log entry is the server's word and decides nothing, so there is no "own echo" shortcut: a message of ours that comes back fails to decrypt and is skipped after three tries, and a commit with our label is applied like any other. The send side (PR C2) will skip exactly the seqs it knows it posted, from the answer to each post.
- **Commits are decided locally.** The contact book and the roster are loaded before the commit is staged (an unavailable contact book is `LookupIncomplete`: nothing staged, nothing frozen). `CommitJudge` then compares epoch and the numbers of added and removed leaves with the envelope, checks removed leaves against the roster, holds a commit that adds a key for a contact that is not pinned (`FROZEN_CONTACT_MISMATCH`), and flags added keys that share a name with another leaf as "new, unverified device" (SEC-18). Merge, or discard and freeze, happens right away, with no suspension in between.
- **Application messages.** A used-up generation (the same ciphertext delivered again, or a retry after one failed attempt consumed it) is skipped at once; any other failure is counted and the message is skipped on the third try. Only application messages are ever skipped. A dead client halts (`Poisoned`) and nothing is stored; the caller restores and counts the failure on the fresh engine.
- **Welcomes.** The join point (our acked seq) is read before the welcome is parsed. A welcome that is unusable or names a contact with an unpinned key is dropped and acknowledged, because parsing consumed its KeyPackage; a join stores the group and its cursor before the welcome is acknowledged.
- **No longer available.** A fetch or ack answered `not_found` (removed, unknown group or withheld, the same answer, ADR-0015) deletes the group's MLS state and keeps it read-only as `REMOVED`. This trades availability for forward secrecy: we cannot tell removal from withholding, and the secrets of a group we may no longer belong to are better deleted than kept; messages already stored stay readable, and a rejoin starts from a new welcome. At most 50 groups are held (`REMOVED` and frozen ones count until `GroupSyncEngine.forget`, which also starts a rejoin); a welcome beyond that stays pending, unparsed, until a slot is freed. The crate refuses a welcome or a commit that would make a group larger than 50 leaves, so the roster (and with it the list of unverified keys) stays within the codec's cap. Added keys are flagged unverified only while their leaf is in the group, so the list stays within the group size.
- Group creation, add/remove and the epoch-conflict retry are PR C2b.

### Send side as built (#22 PR C2a, `SendSide`)
- **Persist before post.** A message is sealed (`encrypt`), stored as the group's one `pending` message inside the snapshot (client message id, payload), and only then posted. The server's `ok` moves it to `lastMsgId`, records its seq in `ownSeqs`, and persists again. A post with an unknown outcome (network, rate limit, full log) stays pending; `resume` and the next `send` post the same envelope again (ADR-0015 makes a repeat safe). The server refusing for good (`envelope_invalid`, `payload_too_large`, `bad_request`) drops it. One message in flight per group; the stored state has a cap, checked before sealing (`Busy`).
- **Freshness before every seal, and before an old message is posted again.** The server's `lastClientMsgId` for this device must equal the stored `lastMsgId` (both absent is fine) or the pending message's id (its answer may have been lost) or the group is frozen as `READ_ONLY_ROLLBACK` and nothing is sent: catching up in an epoch whose ratchets were already used would reuse keys and nonces (owner decision 2026-10-26: "Read-only, then rejoin (Recommended)"). Limit, as in ADR-0002: a restored client is only caught at its next send, and a server that colludes with whoever rolled the state back can replay an old value.
- **Own seqs.** The receive side skips, without a decrypt, only a log entry whose seq **and** the last 32 bytes of whose payload match an accepted post this device recorded (`ownSeqs`). The seq in the server's answer and the sender label are the server's word: the tail ties the entry to our very ciphertext, so a server that names the wrong seq cannot make us skip someone else's message or a commit. At most 100 own posts are tracked; `send` answers `Busy` until a sync has passed them.
- The sender settles an older pending message before it seals a new one; if that does not go through, `send` answers `PendingFirst(outcome)` and the new text is neither sealed nor stored. The freshness check comes first: posting an old pending message into a log that already dropped it would be accepted as new and make a stale state look fresh.

### Commits as built (#22 PR C2b, `CommitSide`)
- **Creating a group** (`createGroup`, 1 to 10 claimed KeyPackages with their device ids): the crate creates the group and prepares the first commit (epoch 0), the group record with the pending commit is stored, and the commit is posted to the create endpoint (a commit at epoch 0 is always a creation). On `ok` it is merged. A refusal forgets the group; an unknown outcome leaves the group and the pending commit, and `resume` posts the same envelope again (the creator repeating its own create gets the first answer, ADR-0015).
- **Add and remove** (`addMembers`, `removeMembers`): freshness is checked first, then the log is read to the end, the crate prepares the commit (held pending inside its state), the pending commit and the MLS state are stored together, and the commit is posted. Removal finds leaves by signature key; a ghost leaf can be named too.
- **Reading before merging.** After `ok` at seq S the committer reads the log up to S-1 with its commit still pending, and only then merges: merging ends the epoch the messages posted ahead of it belong to.
- **Epoch conflict.** The commit is cleared in the crate, the winner is applied by the next read, and a new commit is prepared, three tries at most (then `Unavailable(EPOCH_CONFLICT)`). A refusal for good (`envelope_invalid`, limits) clears the commit and returns `Rejected`; any other failure leaves it pending for `resume`. While a commit is pending, `send` answers `CommitPending`.
- **Limit (server's word).** The seq in the server's answer decides how far the committer reads before it merges. A seq that is too low makes it read too little (some messages of the old epoch are lost to this device); a seq that is too high makes it read its own commit's log entry before it is recorded as its own, which fails and **freezes the group on this device** (`FROZEN_COMMIT_REFUSED` or `FROZEN_GAP`). Both are things a server can already do by withholding or reordering, so it adds no capability. After any stop of that read the accepted commit stays pending, and `CommitPending` or `InFlight` means "call `resume`". A creation is not checked for freshness on `resume`: the server may never have seen the group, and the creator has nothing older to be stale against. `NewMember` and `RemoveTarget` carry a device id and a key that the engine cannot bind to each other: the caller takes both from one contact-book entry (PR D builds them that way and tests a mismatch).
- **Input.** More than 10 changes, no changes, a device id that is not a canonical UUID, a KeyPackage the crate refuses, a signature key that is no leaf, or the own leaf as a removal target are `InvalidRequest`; nothing is stored.


### Bindings (#22 PR B)
- Both FFIs expose the whole lifecycle through the `MlsClient` interface (`:shared`), one method per crate call, with the same closed errors. Results are typed records (`PreparedCommit`, `IncomingMessage` with `Application`/`Commit`, `WelcomeInfo`, `LeafInfo`, `EncryptedMessage`); no call takes a "merge now" shortcut, so the API itself offers no way to apply a commit early; that a commit is merged only after the Delivery Service accepted it is the sync engine's order (PR C, tested there).
- Tokens are `u32` (wasm-bindgen would turn a `u64` into a BigInt), epochs and counts reach the web as JS numbers (exact below 2^53). The web binding takes lists (KeyPackages, leaf indexes) as one length-prefixed `Uint8Array` decoded in `crypto/src/list.rs` (at most 16 items, malformed input is `InvalidInput`); the native binding takes lists directly.
- Decrypted plaintext and keys cross as plain `ByteArray`s and sit in the JVM heap or wasm/JS memory until collected (best effort, like the snapshot key, ADR-0007); the records print sizes only. Accepted: a process that can read that memory has the open chat anyway.
- The message reference is computed in Rust only, so JVM and web give the same value for the same bytes; the contract tests (`MlsGroupContractTest`, run on both) check sender, reference and replay behaviour through the bindings.
- `FakeMlsClient` (`:shared` tests) does not model groups; multi-client tests run against the real client (PR C).

### CLI and end-to-end test (#22 PR D)
- The dev CLI drives the engine through `ChatActions` (`shared`): `chat-create`, `chat-add`, `chat-remove`, `chat-send`, `chat-sync`, `chat-resume`, `chat-welcomes`. It adds no logic of its own. Members to add come from `ClaimKeyPackages`, so the contact book has pinned their keys before the engine sees them; members to remove are found in the same book by name, with each pinned device id and its key together (the precondition of `NewMember`/`RemoveTarget`).
- `chat-sync` prints each decrypted message with its MLS-authenticated sender, because that is how a test shows the round trip; it is the one readable output of the CLI, for test accounts only (threat model, *Dev CLI and real MLS groups*).
- The end-to-end test runs two real members and a third that is added and removed against the real server and database: create, welcome, messages in both directions, add, removal (the removed device finds the group gone), a commit the server refuses (only the creator may remove another member) that leaves nothing pending, and a check that none of the texts appears in the server's, Migrate's, Admin's or Postgres' output.

### Sync rules (the engine: receive side #22 PR C1, send side and freshness PR C2)
- **State lives in the snapshot.** The processed cursor per group, the last two sent client message ids, the frozen/read-only flags and the quarantine list are an opaque `app_state` blob **inside** the sealed snapshot (format v3, set and read through the crate), so one export covers the MLS state and the sync state (owner decision). Done in #22 PR A2: format v3 (v2 is still read), at most 256 KiB, `set_app_state`/`app_state` on the client, plus the #16 export bound (2^24 exports per key, counted in the snapshot) and `rekey`.
- **Order:** fetch, process in strictly consecutive seq order, persist the snapshot with the new cursor, then ack (persist before ack). Send: encrypt, persist (with the message id and ciphertext), post. A send with unknown outcome is retried with the same bytes and the same `clientMsgId` while the epoch is unchanged. The send side skips exactly the seqs it recorded from its own posts, never on a label.
- **Gaps and quarantine:** any jump in seq is a gap: freeze, never quarantine. Only application messages are quarantined, never a commit and never a future-epoch message.
- **Freshness (ADR-0015, ADR-0002):** before **every** encrypt and every `prepare_*`, in every epoch, the server's `lastClientMsgId` must equal the stored last id, or the stored previous id while a send is pending. One in-flight send per group. A detected rollback makes the group **read-only** (owner decision): old messages stay readable, nothing is sent, because catching up in an epoch whose ratchets were already used would reuse keys and nonces that the server, holding both ciphertexts, could exploit. No self-update across epochs. The claim detects a restored client against a server that remembers; a colluding server can replay an old value (ADR-0002).
- **Freeze and rejoin:** a gap, a refused incoming commit, an envelope mismatch and a detected rollback freeze the group for sending. Rejoin is not one click: the stale device is still a server member, so it leaves on the server and forgets the group locally, then a member removes its ghost leaf and adds the device again, or the members make a new group. Transient failures (an incomplete lookup, a failed export, a trap) retry; they never freeze. After a poisoned client or a trap the quarantine record is written from a freshly restored instance.
- **Deleted accounts (#74): detect only** (owner decision). The engine lists leaves whose username or key no longer matches what the server's member list says; a re-registered username with new keys does not match the old pin. Removing them and the UI are M3.

## Consequences
- The server learns nothing new: the envelope is ADR-0015.
- ADR-0007 gains six error codes (amended in this PR); ADR-0002's wording changes (member validation for introduced members, the mismatch warning); ADR-0011's persistence rules gain the app state and the export bound (amended in PR A2).
- Group chats are limited in practice to people one trusts through some member; the UI must say how a member got into a group.
- A 50-leaf group is the cap; ghosts count, so a hostile member can inflate the tree (accepted risk 4 of ADR-0015).
